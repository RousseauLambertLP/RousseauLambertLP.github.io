I'm Louis-Philippe Rousseau-Lambert, a geospatial specialist working on open weather data and geospatial web services at Environment and Climate Change Canada, within the Meteorological Service of Canada.

For several years, I've helped develop, maintain and improve MSC GeoMet: services that make Canadian weather, climate and water data easier to discover, access and use. I enjoy the work between the data and the people using it: building APIs, automating data flows, investigating issues and making documentation more useful.

## What I work on

My day-to-day work brings together Python, MapServer, GDAL/OGR, pygeoapi and Elasticsearch, alongside open standards such as OGC APIs, WMS and WCS. Much of it is about helping frequently updated meteorological datasets fit into reliable geospatial services.

I'm particularly interested in interoperability. Publishing open data is a first step; making it usable in another organization's application is where that work becomes tangible. OGC APIs and WMO's WIS2 ecosystem are especially interesting to me for that reason.

## What I'm exploring

I'm interested in efficient access to large weather datasets, cloud-native geospatial formats, and practical uses of AI and language models with geospatial APIs. I care about keeping the underlying data, rules and validation traceable.

I also share a [technology watch](/en/vigie/) covering open weather data, geospatial software, standards and applied AI. It focuses on what a change means in practice, with links to the original sources.

## Background and life outside work

I graduated from the Université de Sherbrooke with a bachelor's degree in applied geomatics in 2016.

Outside work, I enjoy hiking, camping, photography and sports, especially hockey.
