## The news: a new resolution for open data

**Announcement: October 20, 2026 · Practical importance: high for weather data access.**

ECMWF now distributes its open IFS subset on a **0.1°** latitude–longitude grid alongside the existing 0.25° data, under the CC BY 4.0 licence. Distribution starts with the October 20, 06 UTC forecast cycle. The underlying model is unchanged: this is a new distribution grid for existing forecasts.

**Sources:** [ECMWF announcement](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2026/9-km-free-and-open-data-offers-step-change-localised-forecasts) · [launch cycle confirmation](https://forum.ecmwf.int/t/ecmwfs-9km-data-is-free-and-open-from-tuesday-20th-october/15459).

## What changes in processing pipelines

- Paths use `0p10`; the `0p25` directory remains available.
- At 0.1°, surface and pressure-level fields are split into `-sfc` and `-pl` files. Indexes remain available for selective extraction.
- The 0.1° products arrive roughly two hours after the 0.25° products, with their own WIS2 notifications.
- GRIB packing precision is tuned per parameter. This change applies to open IFS and AIFS data; AIFS distribution remains at 0.25°.

**Technical source:** [ECMWF — Open data](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/datasets/open-data).

## Why it matters

For teams developing weather APIs and geospatial processing pipelines, the additional resolution raises questions about transfer, storage and availability. Indexes and split files offer ways to avoid downloading everything when a workflow needs only a few fields.

**Suggested test:** compare transferred volumes and extraction time at 0.25° and 0.1° for the same variable and a few forecast steps. Also check how GRIB precision affects threshold-sensitive calculations. These are validation recommendations, not observed anomalies.

## A reflection to share

**LinkedIn potential: high.** Making finer forecasts accessible is a step forward; making their volumes practical to use also requires architectural choices. Indexing, selective downloads and WIS2 notifications provide a concrete example of that second part of the work.
