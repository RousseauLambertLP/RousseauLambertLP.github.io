Two recent changes deserve practical attention. The first is a GDAL fix merged this morning; the second is a significant experimental weather-AI development that had not been retained in previous editions. No other significant verified change was found for GeoMet, OGC, WIS2, MapServer, PROJ, pygeoapi, Zarr/xarray/Dask, MCP, or OKF.

## 1. GDAL fixes failed writes of small GRIB2 rasters with NoData

**Date:** November 6, 2026  
**Status:** fix merged into `master`, not yet published in a GDAL release  
**Practical importance:** high for clipping and rewriting GRIB2 data

The documented problem caused `gdal_translate` to fail with `ERROR 1: Error while packing` when writing small GRIB2 rasters containing a NoData value. The reproduced case involved subsets around 10 × 10 pixels or smaller. NoData caused complex packing to be used; a non-fatal error from the group-size reduction algorithm was then propagated as a fatal error.

The fix prevents that incorrect propagation and adds a 10 × 10 raster rewrite test that verifies both the values and exact preservation of NoData. The `DATA_ENCODING=SIMPLE_PACKING` workaround cited in the report allowed the write but did not preserve NoData in that case, so it is not a functionally equivalent replacement.

**Why it matters to you:** weather APIs and pipelines that clip a small area from a GRIB2 field and then re-encode the result can encounter exactly this edge case. Its dependence on size, NoData and encoding can make it difficult to detect when tests cover only larger domains.

**Possible action:** add a GRIB2 output test for small windows with NoData, including complex packing and a read-back check. Wait for a GDAL release containing the commit before treating stable installations as fixed.

**Sources:**

- GDAL issue #15428: <https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/issues/15428>
- GDAL pull request #15474: <https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/pull/15474>
- Merged commit: <https://github.com/OSGeo/gdal/commit/ee0bb1e60edb4e3b21c4567742d2057d7fbbb03e>

## 2. ECMWF moves AIFS-DOP to an experimental real-time prototype

**Date:** November 3, 2026  
**Status:** experimental research prototype; neither an operational system nor a new data release  
**Practical importance:** medium for the evolution of AI numerical weather prediction

ECMWF describes a real-time pipeline, called AIFS-DOP, that initializes a forecast directly from a limited set of observations rather than from a 4D-Var atmospheric analysis. The prototype currently uses a version of AIFS trained on gridded observations with Anemoi, runs on the approximately 30 km N320 grid, and implicitly assimilates the latest 30 hours of observations in six-hour windows before starting the forecast.

The article presents a recent weather case, but ECMWF states that a single case is not a systematic evaluation. This is a move from experiments on historical cases to an experimental real-time pipeline, not evidence that operational assimilation systems or models can be replaced.

**Why it matters to you:** if direct observation-to-forecast approaches mature, they will change dependencies on analyses, latency, provenance requirements and the metadata exposed by data services. Weather APIs will need to distinguish input observations, initialization method, model version, forecast lead time and operational status clearly.

**Possible action:** monitor multi-case evaluations, possible data availability and provenance metadata. No immediate integration is warranted; results should be compared with AIFS and IFS on representative datasets before use.

**Primary source:**

- ECMWF, “From observations to forecasts: bringing AI-DOP into real time”: <https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/aifs-blog/2026/observations-forecasts-bringing-ai-dop-real-time>
